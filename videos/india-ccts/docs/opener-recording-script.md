# Opener script: India's CCTS explainer (to camera, 45–60 seconds)

This plays before the explainer and hands over to its first line, "Imagine two cement plants…". Every fact in it is also in the video.

---

## Main version (about 150 words, 50–60 seconds)

**[1. HOOK: high energy, straight into the lens, no smile yet]**
Four hundred and ninety Indian factories just got a new number to hit.
*(beat)*
Not a production target. A **carbon** target.

**[OPTIONAL INTRO: warm, quick smile]**
*(Only if you want your name in the opener.)* I'm Surya, from ReGeneva.

**[2. TENSION: slow down, lean in slightly]**
Beat that target, and every tonne you save can become a certificate you can **sell**.
Miss it, and you **buy** certificates, or pay compensation at **twice** the average market price for every certificate you're short.

**[3. STAKES: pick up the pace, a hand gesture outward]**
And it doesn't stop at India's border.
Europe's carbon border tax is already live, and the UK has just recognised India's scheme for its own.

**[4. CURIOSITY: pause, slower, a thoughtful look]**
So here's the question every CFO, plant head and sustainability team should be asking:
when the year ends… will your company hold **credits**, or a **bill**?

**[5. PROMISE: energy back up, confident]**
In the next sixteen minutes, I'll break down India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme: what it is, how it works, where it stands today, and where the opportunities are.
Stay till the end, because the last part is about what you should do **now**.

**[6. HANDOVER: smile, slight nod, then hold eye contact for 2 seconds]**
Let's start with two cement plants.

## Short version (about 80 words, 30 seconds)

Four hundred and ninety Indian factories just got a new number to hit. Not a production target. A **carbon** target.
Beat it, and every tonne you save can become a certificate you **sell**. Miss it, and you **pay**.
And with Europe's carbon border tax live, this goes far beyond India.
So, when the year ends, will your company hold **credits**, or a **bill**?
In the next sixteen minutes, I'll show you exactly how India's carbon market works. Let's start with two cement plants.

---

## Pronunciation
- **CCTS**: say "C-C-T-S", letter by letter. The script mostly says "India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme" instead.
- **CBAM**: avoided on purpose. The script says "Europe's carbon border tax".
- **"tonne"**: pronounced "tun".
- **"four hundred and ninety"**: say it in full, not "four-ninety".

## Recording guide (this lets me do a clean studio edit)
**Camera**
- Shoot landscape (16:9), 4K if your phone allows (otherwise 1080p), at 30 fps.
- Put the lens at eye level, about 1–1.5 m away.
- Frame from mid-chest up, with a little space above your head.

**Background**
- Use a plain wall, ideally light grey or off-white, with nothing behind you.
- Sit about 1 m in front of it so I can replace it cleanly with a professional studio background.
- Avoid green clothing, and avoid stripes or fine checks.

**Light**
- Face a window, or a soft light at 45° to one side, slightly above eye level.
- Avoid overhead room lights directly above you; they cause the nose and forehead glare.
- A little blotting paper or matte powder on the nose and forehead before recording helps a lot. I'll still clean up any glare you point out.

**Sound**
- Use a lapel mic if you have one. Otherwise keep the phone within about 1 m.
- A quiet room with soft furnishings works best. Turn off fans and the AC.
- Record **10 seconds of silence** in the room first, so I can remove background noise.

**Takes**
- Record 2–3 full takes. Pause 2 seconds before the first line and after the last line.
- Small stumbles are fine: pause, then restart the sentence. I'll cut them.
- Look into the lens, not at the screen. If you need a teleprompter app, keep it close to the lens.

**What to send me**
1. The video file(s), the original files rather than a WhatsApp-compressed copy.
2. **3–5 photos** for the thumbnail, taken against the same plain wall in the same light:
   - a curious or surprised look
   - a confident look with arms folded
   - pointing to one side
   - a thoughtful look, hand on chin
3. A note marking the glare area, such as the nose or forehead, and anything else you'd like touched up.
