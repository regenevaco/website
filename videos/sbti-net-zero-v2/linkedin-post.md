## Option 1: Main post (recommended)

The net-zero rulebook just got its biggest rewrite since 2021. 🌍

On 11 June 2026, SBTi published Version 2.0 of its Corporate Net-Zero Standard. If your company has, or plans to set, a science-based target, this changes how you'll plan, deliver and report.

We broke it all down in a 9-minute explainer 🎥👇
[YouTube link]

Here are 7 changes every sustainability team should know:

1️⃣ Two company categories
Category A: large companies everywhere, plus medium-sized companies in high-income countries (e.g. ≥ €450m turnover or ≥ 1,000 FTE). Category B gets simpler requirements.

2️⃣ Net-zero targets are now optional
The core is a five-year near-term target cycle. Scope 1 & 2 targets are required for all companies, and scope 3 targets are required for Category A.

3️⃣ Scope 1 and Scope 2 are separated
Each gets its own target covering 100%. Scope 2 emissions targets now use the location-based inventory, or you can set a low-carbon electricity target.

4️⃣ Stricter electricity procurement
Certificates must be matched to consumption within 12 months, in the same grid region, from generators ≤ 15 years old.

5️⃣ Scope 3: the 67% / 90% coverage rules are gone
They're replaced by a significance test: cover every category that makes up ≥ 5% of your scope 3 emissions.

6️⃣ Carbon credits don't count toward targets
They sit in a new "Ongoing Emissions Responsibility" framework. From 2035, Category A companies must support carbon removals equal to ≥ 1% of ongoing scope 1–3 emissions.

7️⃣ More accountability
For Category A: third-party assurance, a board-approved transition plan within 15 months of validation, and annual progress reporting.

📅 Key dates
• Validations under V2.0 open: February 2027
• V2.0 mandatory for new submissions: 1 February 2028
• Already validated? Your targets stay valid for their cycle.

The big shift: less about what you promise, more about what you deliver.
