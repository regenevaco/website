# Voice recording script for Surya's voice clones

Read this once in your natural voice and normal accent, at your usual presenting pace. It should take about 7 minutes. You can record it as one file, or as two files split after Part 5. **Don't try to sound American**: the US-accent clone is built from this same recording.

## How to record
- **Room:** a quiet room with soft furnishings (a bedroom or a carpeted room). No fan or AC, and no music.
- **Microphone:** a USB or lapel mic is best. With a phone, hold it about 20 cm from your mouth, slightly to the side, and use the voice-memo app.
- **Format:** WAV or M4A, at the highest quality setting. Don't apply noise reduction or effects.
- **Delivery:** keep the same energy throughout, the way you'd present to clients. Pause naturally at full stops. If you stumble, pause and re-read that sentence; I'll cut it.
- **Name the file** `Surya-voice-sample-long.wav` (or `.m4a`) and upload it here.

## Part 1: Conversational (about 45 s)
Hello, I'm Surya, and welcome to ReGeneva. Today I want to talk about something that is quietly changing the way businesses think about growth. A few years ago, most companies treated sustainability as a report they published once a year. Now it shows up in board meetings, in investor calls and even in customer contracts. So what changed? Honestly, quite a lot. Let me walk you through it.

## Part 2: Explainer with numbers and acronyms (about 75 s)
India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme, or CCTS, was notified on the twenty-eighth of June, two thousand twenty-three. Under the scheme, the Bureau of Energy Efficiency, BEE, works with the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change, MoEFCC, and the Central Electricity Regulatory Commission, CERC. In October two thousand twenty-five, targets were notified for two hundred and eighty-two plants. By January two thousand twenty-six, the number had grown to four hundred and ninety. One carbon credit certificate equals one tonne of carbon dioxide equivalent. If a plant reduces its emissions intensity from zero point eight two to zero point seven seven, the difference is roughly six percent. That may sound small, but across a million tonnes of cement, it adds up to thirty thousand tonnes.

## Part 3: Questions, emphasis and emotion (about 45 s)
Is this good news for industry? In many ways, yes. But is it enough? That's the real question. Some experts are optimistic; others are cautious. Here's what I think: the companies that move early will have a genuine advantage. The ones that wait will pay for it later, quite literally. Remember this: data first, decisions second. Without reliable data, nothing else works.

## Part 4: Phonetic variety (about 45 s)
The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog. She sells sea shells by the seashore, while thirty-three thousand thrilled thinkers thought through the theory. Measure, verify, report, and repeat. Vision, pleasure, treasure, and decision. Water, butter, better, little, bottle. Rural, world, girl, and squirrel. Think, thank, those, these, and through.

## Part 5: Closing, warm and confident (about 30 s)
To sum up, carbon markets are not just about compliance. They are about competitiveness, efficiency and long-term value. If you found this useful, subscribe to ReGeneva and share it with your team. Thank you for watching, and I'll see you in the next one.

## Part 6: Sustainability terms and jargon (about 3 min)
*Say each acronym the way you normally would in a client meeting, whether letter by letter (G-H-G) or as a word (C-BAM as "see-bam"). That's the pronunciation the clones will learn.*

**6a. Frameworks, standards and ratings**
- Our GHG emissions inventory follows the GHG Protocol and ISO fourteen-zero-six-four.
- We report under GRI, SASB and the IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards: IFRS S1 and IFRS S2, issued by the ISSB.
- TCFD shaped climate disclosure; TNFD now does the same for nature.
- Indian listed companies file the BRSR, and the top one thousand need BRSR Core assurance.
- In Europe, the CSRD and the ESRS require double materiality, alongside the EU Taxonomy and SFDR.
- Investors also look at CDP scores, MSCI ESG ratings, Sustainalytics, EcoVadis, and the S&P Global Corporate Sustainability Assessment, the CSA that feeds into the DJSI.

**6b. Targets and climate strategy**
- The Science Based Targets initiative, SBTi, validates near-term and net-zero targets aligned with one point five degrees Celsius.
- Scope 1 covers direct emissions, Scope 2 covers purchased electricity, and Scope 3 covers the value chain, from purchased goods to the use of sold products.
- Scope 2 can be reported as location-based or market-based.
- Decarbonization means cutting emissions at source, through energy efficiency, electrification, fuel switching and renewable energy.
- A credible transition plan and a climate risk assessment, the CRA, cover physical risk, transition risk and scenario analysis.
- An internal carbon price, a marginal abatement cost curve and a decarbonization roadmap help prioritise investment.

**6c. Carbon markets and policy**
- Carbon credits can come from avoidance, reduction or removal projects.
- Removals include afforestation, biochar, direct air capture, DAC, and BECCS.
- CCUS stands for carbon capture, utilisation and storage.
- Under Article six point two and Article six point four of the Paris Agreement, countries trade ITMOs.
- The ICVCM's Core Carbon Principles and VCMI's Claims Code guide high-integrity voluntary markets. Aviation uses CORSIA.
- Compliance markets include the EU ETS and India's CCTS.
- The EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism, CBAM, applies to steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, hydrogen and electricity.
- Nationally Determined Contributions, NDCs, are reviewed at each COP under the UNFCCC.

**6d. Energy, products and operations**
- Renewable energy is procured through PPAs, green tariffs, RECs and I-RECs, and companies join RE100.
- A life cycle assessment, LCA, produces a product carbon footprint, PCF, or an environmental product declaration, EPD.
- The circular economy, extended producer responsibility, EPR, and waste-to-energy reduce landfill.
- Water stewardship, biodiversity, nature-positive goals and a just transition are all rising priorities.

**6e. Governance, risk and integrity**
- Greenwashing, greenhushing and green-wishing all damage trust.
- That's why limited assurance, reasonable assurance and third-party verification matter.
- ESG due diligence, stakeholder engagement, materiality assessment, supply chain traceability, and human rights under the UN Guiding Principles are now board-level topics, alongside the UN SDGs and CSR spending under Section one-thirty-five of the Companies Act.
